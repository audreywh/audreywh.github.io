About

I'm an Applied & Engineering Physics student at Cornell University, Class of 2029, with interests in experimental quantum computing, photonics, quantum electronics, and hardware engineering.

Most of what I've learned has come from building things. I've built the pulse amplifier for a nitrogen laser, electromagnets and a coil-winding machine for Cornell Hyperloop, and gate-level logic on an FPGA. On the theory side, I study quantum error-correction decoders with the Cornell Quantum Computing Association, and I've worked on fast radio burst data as a Nexus Scholar in the Extreme Astrophysics Group.

I also help teach Lasers & Photonics as a laboratory assistant.

Outside of academics, I enjoy playing tennis, exploring new places, and finding creative outlets through photography and design. I also love learning new things and taking on projects that challenge me to think differently.
